The Saas Valley offers more than 350km of marked trails, ranging from leisurely valley walks to demanding high-altitude routes. The scenery — dominated by 13 four-thousanders and the Fee Glacier — is spectacular at every turn, and trails are well-signposted with the classic Swiss yellow hiking markers that make navigation straightforward even for first-time visitors to the Alps.

## Trail Difficulty Guide

Switzerland uses a nationally standardised trail marking system that you'll encounter on every signpost and map in the Saas Valley. Understanding the system before you set out makes route planning straightforward.

**Yellow sign — Wanderweg (Hiking Trail)**
Easy trails on paved, gravel, or wide dirt paths with minimal elevation change. No special equipment required. Suitable for all ages and fitness levels, including families with young children and visitors in everyday footwear.

**White-red-white — Bergweg (Mountain Trail)**
Marked trails on mountain terrain that may include steeper sections, narrow paths, and some scrambling. A reasonable level of fitness is required, and you should wear proper hiking boots with ankle support. Some sure-footedness is needed, particularly on exposed sections.

**White-blue-white — Alpinweg (Alpine Route)**
High-altitude or technically demanding routes requiring mountaineering experience, appropriate equipment (including a helmet, harness, and rope in some cases), and the ability to navigate in challenging conditions. These routes are not suitable for casual hikers and should only be attempted with a qualified guide if you are unfamiliar with Alpine terrain.

## Practical Tips

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get your map before you go">
    Download the **SaasFee app** (available for iOS and Android) for offline trail maps and GPS tracking, or pick up a printed 1:25,000 trail map from the tourist office on the main street. Having a map you trust makes it easy to adjust your route on the fly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start early">
    Set out in the morning, ideally before 9am on longer routes. Alpine afternoons frequently bring cloud build-up and afternoon thunderstorms, especially in July and August. Starting early means you complete the high sections of any route before weather deteriorates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pack for mountain conditions">
    Carry at least 1.5 litres of water per person — mountain springs exist but should be treated if you plan to drink from them. Pack sun cream (UV levels are high at altitude), sunglasses, a warm mid-layer, and a waterproof jacket. Mountain weather can change from sunshine to hail within the hour.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register your route for remote walks">
    If you are tackling a remote or high-altitude route alone, register your planned itinerary with **Swiss Alpine Rescue**. In an emergency, dial **1414** for the Swiss Air Rescue helicopter service (Rega). The SaasFee tourist office can also advise on current trail conditions and guide recommendations.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Always check trail conditions before setting out, especially after rain or a period of unsettled weather. Some high-altitude trails, particularly white-blue-white alpine routes, may be closed due to residual snowfall until **July** or later in a heavy snow year. The tourist office website publishes daily trail condition updates during the summer season.
</Warning>
