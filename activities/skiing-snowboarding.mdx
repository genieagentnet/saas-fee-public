Saas-Fee is home to one of the Alps' finest glacier ski areas, stretching from the village at 1800m up to 3600m at Mittelallalin — the world's highest revolving restaurant. With 100km of marked pistes and reliable snow from October to May, plus summer glacier skiing, it's a world-class destination for skiers and snowboarders of every level.

## The Ski Area

Saas-Fee's glacier ski area punches well above its weight for a village of its size. The combination of high altitude, a compact car-free resort, and a well-connected lift network makes it one of the most snowsure destinations in the Swiss Alps.

## Piste Map Overview

Saas-Fee's ski area divides naturally into three distinct zones, each with its own character and best suited to different abilities.

**Lower Mountain — 1800–2400m (Village to Morenia)**
The gentler lower slopes are perfect for building confidence. Blue and red runs wind through this zone, connecting the village lifts to the mid-mountain. Morenia is the hub here, home to the snowpark and a cluster of mountain restaurants.

**Middle Mountain — 2800–3000m (Felskinn & Britannia)**
Accessed via the Felskinn cable car or the Alpin Express gondola, the middle mountain opens up a network of red and black runs with long, satisfying descents. The Britannia area rewards skiers with superb views across the glacier and the chance to tackle more technical terrain.

**Glacier Zone — 3200–3600m (Mittelallalin)**
The crown of the ski area. Reached by the Metro Alpin underground funicular from Felskinn, the glacier plateau at Mittelallalin offers wide, open glacier pistes and the most reliable snow on the mountain. This is also where summer skiing takes place, and where you'll find the famous revolving restaurant.

## Skiing for Every Level

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Beginners">
    Saas-Fee is an excellent choice for first-timers and those in the early stages of learning. The car-free village means you can walk to the lifts safely, and the designated learning areas are well set up.

    - **Nursery slope at Spielboden** — a wide, gentle piste ideal for your first turns, served by a beginner drag lift
    - **Ski school** — the Swiss Ski School (Skischule Saas-Fee) runs group lessons for adults and children from age three, with English-speaking instructors available throughout the season
    - **Recommended runs** — start with the blue runs at Morenia before progressing to the easy reds on the lower mountain once you have your snow legs
    - **Top tip** — book a multi-day lesson package to make the most rapid progress; most learners are skiing independently on blues by day three
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Intermediate">
    Intermediates are the biggest winners in Saas-Fee. The mountain's layout rewards confident blue and red run skiers with long, varied descents and the freedom to explore all three zones.

    - **Längfluh** — one of the resort's most popular red runs, offering a long top-to-bottom descent with consistently enjoyable terrain and great glacier views
    - **Felskinn reds** — a network of satisfying red runs in the middle mountain zone, quieter than the main Morenia area on busy weekends
    - **Glacier access** — intermediates can comfortably ski the open glacier pistes at Mittelallalin, particularly enjoyable in the early morning when the snow surface is at its best
    - **Morenia Snowpark** — if you want to try park skiing for the first time, Morenia's beginner-friendly features are a great introduction
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Advanced & Expert">
    Expert skiers will find Saas-Fee's black runs demanding, and the off-piste and ski touring opportunities are genuinely world-class.

    - **Hänfige** — the resort's most notorious black run, a steep and often mogulled descent that separates the experts from the rest
    - **Off-piste** — the open faces around Felskinn and the glacier offer excellent off-piste skiing after fresh snowfall; always hire a local guide for routes away from marked pistes
    - **Ski touring** — the high-altitude terrain around Saas-Fee is a touring paradise. The classic Haute Route from Chamonix ends here, and shorter tours to the Britannia Hütte or Allalinhorn are popular with fit, experienced tourers
    - **Freeride** — the resort hosts occasional freeride competitions, and the surrounding peaks — including several four-thousanders — provide serious challenge for the most experienced off-piste skiers
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Ski School & Rentals

**Swiss Ski School Saas-Fee (Skischule Saas-Fee)** is the resort's main ski school and has been teaching in the valley for decades. Instructors are qualified to Swiss national standards, and English-speaking lessons are available across all levels.

- **Group lessons** — run daily throughout the season, typically in the morning. Groups are split by ability and age, keeping instruction relevant and progress rapid
- **Private lessons** — available for individuals, couples, or families who want focused one-to-one instruction. Recommended for beginners who want to progress quickly, or advanced skiers working on specific technique
- **Children's ski school** — operates from age three with dedicated instructors trained in teaching young children. A ski kindergarten service is also available for the youngest visitors

**Equipment rental** is available from multiple shops in the village. Most rental outlets stock modern carving skis, snowboards, boots, helmets, and poles for all sizes. Pre-book online for guaranteed availability and often a small discount on walk-in rates.

## Summer Glacier Skiing

One of Saas-Fee's most remarkable features is its summer ski season. The Fee Glacier remains open for skiing from **July through to late October**, making Saas-Fee one of only a handful of resorts in the world to offer genuine summer skiing on snow rather than artificial surfaces.

The summer ski area centres on the Mittelallalin glacier plateau at 3200–3500m. The glacier is popular with **European national ski racing teams**, who use Saas-Fee as a training base during the summer months — you may well find yourself sharing the mountain with elite athletes preparing for the coming World Cup season.

The summer ski area is open to the public with a standard lift pass. Mornings are the best time to ski; the glacier typically closes to skiers by early afternoon as the sun softens the snow surface. Summer skiing is a genuinely extraordinary experience — the combination of warm sunshine, crisp glacier air, and pristine high-altitude snow is unlike anything available on a typical winter ski holiday.

<Tip>
  For the very best snow conditions on the glacier, aim to be on the Mittelallalin lifts as soon as they open in the morning. The glacier surface is firmest and fastest in the early hours before the sun gets to work — and the crowds are thinner too. By midday, especially in spring and summer, the top layer softens considerably.
</Tip>
