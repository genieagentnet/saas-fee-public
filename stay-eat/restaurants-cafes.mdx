Eating in Saas-Fee is one of the pleasures of a visit. The village has a diverse dining scene — traditional Swiss mountain cuisine sits alongside international menus, casual après-ski bars, and high-end hotel restaurants. Mountain huts on the slopes serve warming lunches with spectacular views.

## Traditional Swiss Dishes to Try

Make the most of your time in the Alps by working your way through the classics. These are the dishes that define Swiss mountain cooking — hearty, warming, and deeply satisfying after a day on the slopes.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Raclette">
    One of Switzerland's most iconic dishes. A half-wheel of Raclette cheese is melted under a grill or over an open flame, then scraped directly onto your plate alongside boiled potatoes, pickled gherkins, and pearl onions. Simple, communal, and utterly delicious — order it at least once during your stay.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Fondue">
    A pot of melted cheese — typically a blend of Gruyère and Emmental — kept bubbling over a small burner at your table. You dip cubes of crusty bread into the cheese using long forks. Fondue is perfect for groups on a cold evening and is widely available throughout the village. Book ahead at dedicated fondue restaurants, especially in peak season.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Rösti">
    A crispy, pan-fried potato cake that has become a Swiss national staple. Rösti is made from grated, par-cooked potatoes, pressed into a skillet and fried until golden on both sides. It is often served as a side dish or topped with a fried egg, melted cheese, or smoked salmon for a more substantial meal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Cholera">
    A traditional Valais pastry pie and a true local speciality of the Saas Valley. The name refers to a historical legend, but the dish itself is entirely appetising — a golden, flaky pastry shell filled with a warming mixture of potato, leek, cheese, and onion. Look for it on menus at more traditional and locally run restaurants.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Älplermagronen">
    Switzerland's answer to alpine comfort food. Älplermagronen (literally "Alpine herdsman's macaroni") is a baked dish of pasta, potatoes, cream, and melted cheese, often topped with crispy fried onions. It is traditionally served with a portion of warm apple sauce on the side — the sweet-savoury combination is surprisingly addictive.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Dining on the Mountain

Mountain restaurants are an integral part of the ski day in Saas-Fee, and some are worth visiting in their own right. Take a long lunch break, soak up the views from a sunny terrace, and refuel properly before heading back to the slopes.

- **Revolving Restaurant at Mittelallalin (3,500 m)** — one of the world's highest revolving restaurants, offering 360° panoramic views of the surrounding glaciers and peaks. The full rotation takes around 45 minutes, making lunch here a genuine experience. Arrive early or book ahead during busy periods.
- **Morenia** — a well-positioned mid-mountain restaurant with a large sun terrace, perfect for a leisurely lunch between ski runs. Serves classic mountain dishes and hot drinks.
- **Spielboden** — situated at 2,450 m and accessible by cable car, Spielboden has a terrace with outstanding views toward the Allalinhorn. It is also a popular spot in summer for hikers exploring the high-alpine trails.
- **Britannia Hut (SAC)** — a Swiss Alpine Club mountain hut at over 3,000 m, accessible by ski or on foot in summer. A favourite destination for ski tourers and summer mountaineers; it offers simple, hearty meals and overnight accommodation in a spectacular high-alpine setting.

## Village Dining

Back in the village, you have plenty of options whatever the occasion or your appetite.

- **Fine dining** in hotel restaurants features seasonal menus, local produce, and accomplished cooking — ideal for a special evening out
- **Casual pizzerias and Italian restaurants** offer reliable, crowd-pleasing food at reasonable prices and are popular with families
- **Swiss fondue and raclette restaurants** are the go-to choice for a classic alpine evening — book ahead, as the best spots fill up quickly in peak season
- **Cafés and bakeries** are perfect for a leisurely breakfast, a mid-morning coffee, or an afternoon slice of cake after a day on the slopes
- **Après-ski bars** typically serve snacks, sharing plates, and substantial bar food alongside drinks — the natural bridge between the mountain and the dinner table

## Dietary Requirements

Saas-Fee's restaurants are accustomed to catering for a wide range of dietary needs.

- **Vegetarian** options are widely available across almost all restaurants and mountain huts — Switzerland's dairy-rich cuisine lends itself well to meat-free eating
- **Vegan and gluten-free** options are increasingly common, though availability varies between establishments; call ahead if this is important to you
- **Allergies** — always inform your server clearly of any food allergies or intolerances before ordering; staff are generally well-informed and helpful
- **Halal and kosher** options are limited within the village; guests with strict dietary requirements may wish to self-cater for some meals

## Meal Times

Swiss dining follows a fairly consistent daily rhythm. Arriving outside these windows can mean limited service or kitchen closures, particularly at smaller establishments.

| Meal | Typical Hours |
|------|--------------|
| Breakfast | 07:30 – 10:00 |
| Lunch | 12:00 – 14:00 |
| Dinner | 18:30 – 21:30 |
| Mountain restaurants (ski season) | From 09:00 |
| Après-ski bars | Food often served until late |
