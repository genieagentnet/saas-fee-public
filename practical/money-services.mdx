Saas-Fee is a well-equipped mountain resort with all the essential services you need for a comfortable stay. Despite its remote Alpine setting, the village has reliable banking facilities, strong mobile coverage, and a well-staffed tourist information office. This guide covers currency, banking, internet access, postal services, and the key emergency contacts you should save before you arrive.

## Currency & Payment

Switzerland uses the **Swiss Franc (CHF)** as its official currency. A few things to know before you arrive:

- **Euros are widely accepted** in hotels, restaurants, ski rental shops, and most tourist-facing businesses — but you will almost always receive your change back in Swiss Francs. If you're paying for a CHF 18 coffee with a €20 note, expect your change in CHF.
- **Credit and debit cards** — Visa and Mastercard are accepted almost everywhere in the village, from supermarkets and restaurants to ski hire shops and the ticket office. Contactless payment (including Apple Pay and Google Pay) is common and widely supported.
- **American Express** is accepted at many hotels and larger restaurants but is less universally supported at smaller shops and mountain restaurants. Carry a Visa or Mastercard as your primary card.
- **Cash remains useful** for smaller purchases, mountain huts, and any transactions at smaller establishments that don't accept cards. Having CHF 100–200 in cash is sensible for a week's visit.
- **Currency exchange** is available at the Raiffeisen Bank branch and the post office in the village. Airport and city exchange bureaux generally offer better rates than resort locations — exchange before you arrive if possible.

## ATMs & Banks

You won't need to worry about running out of cash in Saas-Fee — the village has reliable ATM access throughout the year.

- **Bancomats (ATMs)** are available at multiple locations in the village centre. They accept all major international debit and credit cards and dispense Swiss Francs. Most machines support English-language menus.
- **Raiffeisen Bank** has a branch in Saas-Fee, offering full banking services for account holders. Branch banking hours are typically **Monday–Friday, 09:00–12:00 and 14:00–17:00**.
- **Outside banking hours**, use the 24-hour ATMs — these operate around the clock regardless of whether the bank branch is open.
- **International withdrawal fees** may apply depending on your home bank. Check with your bank before travelling, and consider a travel-friendly card (such as Wise or Revolut) to reduce currency conversion charges.

## Mobile & Internet

Staying connected in Saas-Fee is easy, whether you're streaming trail maps on the slope or checking tomorrow's weather forecast.

- **Mobile coverage:** all three major Swiss mobile networks — **Swisscom, Salt, and Sunrise** — provide coverage in the village and across most of the ski area. 4G/LTE is the standard throughout the resort. Coverage can thin out in remote off-piste terrain at the edges of the ski area, but on marked pistes you should have a reliable signal.
- **EU roaming:** if you're travelling from an EU country with a standard roaming plan, your data and calls should work normally in Switzerland. Note that Switzerland is not part of the EU, so some older or budget plans may charge roaming fees — confirm with your provider before travelling.
- **Wi-Fi:** free Wi-Fi is available in virtually all hotels, apartments, and chalets, as well as in most cafés and restaurants in the village centre. The village centre itself has public Wi-Fi hotspots in several locations.
- **Mountain restaurants:** many mid-mountain restaurants and the top-station cafeteria at Mittelallalin offer guest Wi-Fi — useful for sharing those summit photos without eating into your mobile data.

## Post Office

The Swiss Post (**Die Post**) office in Saas-Fee provides a full range of postal and parcel services, as well as basic financial services.

- **Services:** sending letters and parcels (including ski equipment home), registered mail, and PostFinance money services
- **Typical opening hours:** Monday–Friday, **08:30–12:00 and 14:00–17:30**
- **Parcels and equipment:** if you've bought ski gear or gifts and don't want to carry them home, the post office can arrange shipping to most international destinations
- **Swiss Post tracking** is available online at post.ch for any tracked items you send or receive during your stay

Note that post office hours may vary during public holidays and at the beginning and end of the ski season. Check locally on arrival if you need to use the post office at an unusual time.

## Tourist Information Office

The **Saas-Fee Tourism office** should be your first stop on arrival — and it's worth revisiting throughout your stay, as conditions, events, and opportunities change quickly in a mountain resort.

- **Services:** trail and piste maps, accommodation advice, event calendars, ski pass information, guided activity bookings, and general visitor assistance
- **Location:** in the village centre, a short walk from the main lift stations
- **Opening hours:** open daily during the main season (winter and summer); reduced hours during the shoulder seasons
- **Languages:** staff speak English, German, and French — and are well used to helping international visitors
- **Contact:** visit in person, or reach the office by phone at **+41 27 958 18 58**

The tourist office can also provide specific information on accessibility facilities, current trail conditions, and local regulations for activities such as ski touring and snowshoeing.

## Emergency Contacts

Save these numbers before you head into the mountains. In a genuine emergency, seconds matter.

| Service | Number |
|---|---|
| **Emergency (all services)** | 112 |
| **Police** | 117 |
| **Ambulance** | 144 |
| **Mountain Rescue / REGA** | 1414 |
| **Fire** | 118 |
| **Saas-Fee Tourist Office** | +41 27 958 18 58 |

- **112** is the pan-European emergency number and works from any mobile phone, even without a SIM card or signal on your home network.
- **1414 (REGA)** is the Swiss Air Rescue service. When calling from the mountain, try to give your location as precisely as possible — the nearest piste marker number, lift station, or GPS coordinates from your phone.
- **The REGA app** (free, available on iOS and Android) can automatically transmit your GPS coordinates to the rescue centre when you make an emergency call — worth downloading before your trip.

<Warning>
  Mountain rescue operations in Switzerland may incur significant costs for uninsured visitors. Helicopter evacuation alone can cost several thousand Swiss Francs. We strongly recommend purchasing travel insurance that explicitly includes **mountain rescue and helicopter evacuation** before your trip. Check the small print carefully — some standard travel policies exclude skiing or off-piste activity.
</Warning>
