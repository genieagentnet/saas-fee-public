Saas-Fee's events calendar reflects both its sporting heritage and its deep Walliser cultural roots. From world-class trail races and ski competitions to traditional Alpine festivals and Christmas markets, there's always something happening in the village. Whether you're planning your trip around a specific event or simply hoping to catch some local atmosphere, the calendar below will help you make the most of what Saas-Fee has to offer throughout the year.

## Winter Events

The winter season runs from late November through to April, and the event programme is as varied as the terrain. Alongside skiing and snowboarding, you'll find cultural evenings, festive celebrations, and competitive events that draw spectators from across Europe.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Christmas Market (December)">
    The Christmas market transforms the village centre into one of the most atmospheric festive settings in the Swiss Alps. Local craftspeople and food producers set up stalls selling hand-carved wooden items, local cheeses, dried meats, and seasonal specialities. The scent of mulled wine (*Glühwein*) drifts through the lantern-lit lanes as the mountains rise silently behind the rooftops.

    Candlelit processions wind through the village in the evenings, and live folk music performances add to the warmth of the occasion. It's a genuinely community-led event — not a commercial import — and that authenticity makes it one of the highlights of the Saas-Fee year.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="New Year's Eve">
    New Year's Eve in Saas-Fee is a spectacle set against one of the world's most dramatic natural backdrops. Fireworks burst above the Fee Glacier as the clock strikes midnight, illuminating the ice and the surrounding peaks in colour. Torchlight ski descents from the mountain bring columns of light slowly down the slopes to the valley floor, where celebrations in the village square continue well into the night.

    If you're planning to be here for New Year's Eve, book your accommodation many months in advance — this is one of the most sought-after dates in the resort calendar.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ski Races & Freestyle Events">
    Saas-Fee's competition pistes host regular ski racing events through the winter season, ranging from local club races to more widely attended competitions. The village's long ski season — bolstered by glacier skiing from autumn — means that early-season training races sometimes attract national squad athletes preparing for the competition calendar.

    The **Morenia Snowpark** is the venue for freestyle skiing and snowboard events, including slopestyle and rail jam competitions. The snowpark's features are regularly updated throughout the season, and competition events are typically free to watch from the viewing areas alongside the park.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Valais Folklore Evening">
    Traditional Valais folklore evenings are a highlight for visitors who want to experience Walliser culture first-hand. These evenings — typically organised by hotels for their guests but open to village visitors — bring together traditional music, folk dancing, regional food and wine, and performances by local groups in traditional dress (*Tracht*).

    The local brass band and yodelling groups are regular performers. If you haven't encountered alpine folk music before, a folklore evening is the perfect introduction — it's warmly presented and genuinely festive rather than staged.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Summer Events

Saas-Fee's summer season is anchored by trail running, glacier activities, and the rich cultural traditions of the Valais agricultural calendar. The village comes alive in a different way to winter — lighter, greener, and shaped by the rhythms of the alpine pastoral year.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Saas-Fee Trail Running Races (July–August)">
    Trail running has become one of Saas-Fee's signature summer activities, and the race calendar reflects that. Events range from short family-friendly runs with minimal elevation gain to serious ultra-distance challenges that take runners deep into the high alpine terrain surrounding the village.

    International participants arrive each summer, giving the race days a genuine festival atmosphere. Even if you're not racing, the finish-line energy in the village square on race days is something to experience. Routes make use of the resort's extensive trail network, and many stages pass close to the glacier — a dramatic backdrop for any race.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Fee Glacier Opening Day">
    The official opening of summer glacier skiing — usually in early July — is a community celebration as much as a sporting milestone. The glacier lifts have typically been running for weeks before the official summer opening, but the ceremony marks the start of the main summer ski season and brings together resort staff, local athletes, and early-season visitors.

    It's a good day to be on the mountain: demonstration runs, informal competitions, and a festive atmosphere at the glacier station make it one of the more unusual summer celebrations you'll find at any European resort.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Swiss National Day (1 August)">
    The first of August is Switzerland's National Day, and it's celebrated throughout the country with an enthusiasm that belies the Swiss reputation for understatement. In Saas-Fee, you can expect bonfires on the mountain, fireworks over the village, and community gatherings in the square.

    The atmosphere is inclusive and welcoming to visitors — National Day in a Swiss mountain village is one of those experiences that's hard to replicate elsewhere. Children carry lanterns in evening processions, speeches are made, and the sense of community pride is palpable. It's a national holiday, so some services run reduced hours.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Alpaufzug — Alpine Herding Festival (June)">
    The **Alpaufzug** is one of the most purely traditional events in the Saas Valley calendar. Each June, cattle are driven from the valley floor up to the high summer pastures in a colourful procession that has taken place for centuries. The cows are decorated with garlands of flowers and large ceremonial bells, their owners dressed in traditional Walliser costume.

    The procession moves through the village and up into the alpine meadows above — a living connection between the modern resort and the farming traditions that built it. It's a genuinely moving occasion, and one of the clearest reminders that Saas-Fee is not just a ski resort, but a community with deep roots in the alpine pastoral landscape.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Open Air Concerts & Village Festivals (July–August)">
    Through the height of summer, the village square hosts a regular programme of open air concerts and village festivals. Musical styles range from local folk and brass band performances to international acts brought in by the resort during peak season. Many performances are free to attend and take place in the early evening, giving them a relaxed, social character.

    Village festival days typically combine music with local food and drink stalls, craft markets, and children's activities. Check the events board at the Tourist Office or the resort's website for the current summer programme on arrival.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Event Planning Tips

Planning your visit around a specific Saas-Fee event takes a little advance preparation, but the effort is well worth it.

- **Book accommodation early.** Major events — particularly New Year's Eve, the Christmas market, and trail race weekends — fill the village's accommodation months in advance. The earlier you book, the better your options and rates.
- **Check dates before committing.** Many events don't have fixed calendar dates and shift by a week or two from year to year depending on conditions, the alpine calendar, or organiser decisions. Always confirm the exact date on the Saas-Fee Tourism website before booking flights or transfers.
- **Understand what's ticketed.** Some events — particularly organised folklore evenings and certain concerts — require advance tickets or are hotel-guest-only. Others, including the Christmas market, National Day celebrations, and the Alpaufzug, are free open-air village events. The Tourist Office can tell you what's ticketed in any given season.
- **Allow time either side.** Arriving a day before a major event and leaving a day after gives you breathing room if travel is disrupted, and means you won't miss the best of the pre- or post-event atmosphere.
