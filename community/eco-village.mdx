Saas-Fee was one of the world's first resort villages to commit to a car-free policy — back in 1951. Today, that pioneering spirit lives on in the village's status as part of the UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and its ongoing commitment to sustainable tourism and environmental conservation. Choosing to visit Saas-Fee is, in itself, a more sustainable choice than many alpine alternatives — and the village actively encourages you to deepen that commitment through how you travel, stay, and explore.

## Car-Free Village

Saas-Fee has been car-free since 1951 — a decision made decades before sustainability became a mainstream concern, and one that has defined the village's character ever since. Private motor vehicles are not permitted in the village centre. Instead, you leave your car at the large parking facilities at the village entrance and continue on foot, by electric taxi, or by the small electric buses that shuttle guests and luggage through the lanes.

The effect of this single policy is profound. The air in Saas-Fee is clean in a way that you notice immediately after driving up through the valley. The streets are quiet. Children walk freely. The soundscape is dominated by cowbells, birdsong, and the distant hum of the cable cars rather than engines and horns. It is an environment that prioritises people over vehicles — and it works.

Electric taxis and e-buses handle local transport needs, including luggage transfers between the car parks and accommodation, and the flat village layout makes walking everywhere both practical and pleasant.

## UNESCO Biosphere Reserve

The broader Saas Valley and its surrounding alpine landscape are recognised as an area of outstanding natural value within the **UNESCO Biosphere Reserve** framework. This designation is not simply an honorary title — it carries active obligations around biodiversity protection, sustainable land use, and the preservation of traditional farming practices that have shaped the landscape for centuries.

The biosphere designation protects the full alpine ecosystem: the high pastures used for summer grazing, the rocky ridgelines and glacial moraines above, the river systems running through the valley floor, and the forests and meadows in between. Traditional agricultural practices — including the seasonal movement of cattle to high summer pastures (the Alpaufzug) — are recognised as integral to the health of this ecosystem rather than as obstacles to conservation.

As a visitor, you are entering a living, protected landscape. That context shapes how you should engage with it: with curiosity, respect, and care.

## Renewable Energy

Saas-Fee has invested heavily in solar and hydroelectric power generation. The village and ski area draw a significant and growing proportion of their energy needs from renewable sources, with hydroelectric power — generated by the significant water resources of the surrounding mountains and glaciers — forming the backbone of the local energy supply.

Solar energy installations on rooftops and mountain infrastructure contribute additional generation capacity, particularly during the long daylight hours of the summer season. Several new buildings in the village are constructed to **Minergie** standards — Switzerland's rigorous low-energy building certification — reducing heating and cooling demands and setting a benchmark for new development across the resort.

The ski area's lifts and snowmaking systems, which are among the most energy-intensive elements of any mountain resort, are operated with renewable energy targets that the resort continues to work towards and refine.

## Glacier Conservation

The Fee Glacier is one of the most accessible glaciers in the Alps and one of Saas-Fee's most extraordinary natural assets. Descending from the high plateau between the Allalinhorn, Alphubel, and Feekopf, it reaches closer to a permanent settlement than almost any other glacier in Europe — and you can walk to its edge from the village in under two hours.

Like glaciers worldwide, the Fee Glacier faces serious challenges from climate change. The resort works in partnership with glaciologists and environmental scientists to monitor glacier retreat systematically, contributing to the broader scientific understanding of alpine ice dynamics. On the most sensitive sections of the summer ski area, **reflective covers** are deployed during the warmest months to slow surface melting — a technique that has proven effective at reducing seasonal ice loss in critical zones.

Understanding the glacier's vulnerability is part of what makes visiting it meaningful. You are not simply consuming a scenic backdrop; you are encountering a landscape under pressure, being actively cared for by the community that depends on it.

## Responsible Tourism Principles

Saas-Fee asks its visitors to engage with the landscape and community in ways that protect both for future generations. These principles are straightforward — and following them costs nothing.

## Certifications & Awards

Saas-Fee's sustainability credentials are formally recognised at multiple levels:

- **Swisstainable:** The village is recognised under Switzerland Tourism's **Swisstainable** programme, which certifies tourism destinations and providers that meet measurable sustainability standards across environmental management, social responsibility, and economic resilience. This certification is not self-awarded — it is independently assessed and regularly reviewed.
- **Car-Free Resort Recognition:** Saas-Fee is internationally recognised as a pioneering car-free alpine resort and is part of networks of vehicle-free destinations across Europe. Its 70-plus-year track record of car-free operation is cited as a model for sustainable resort development.
- **Environmental Management Programmes:** The resort and municipality operate ongoing environmental management programmes covering waste reduction, water management, energy efficiency, and biodiversity protection. These programmes are integrated into the planning and operation of the resort rather than existing as separate sustainability add-ons.

The combination of these certifications and the village's lived commitment to sustainability — expressed in its daily operations, community decisions, and long-term planning — makes Saas-Fee one of the most genuinely eco-conscious mountain destinations in Europe.
