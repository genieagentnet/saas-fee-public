Saas-Fee's community spirit is one of its greatest strengths. Whether you're a long-term visitor who returns every year, a seasonal worker, or a new permanent resident, there are many ways to engage with village life beyond the ski slopes and hiking trails. Saas-Fee is a real community of real people — and those who make the effort to connect with it, rather than simply passing through it, consistently describe their relationship with the village as one of the most rewarding aspects of their time here.

## Community Events & Volunteering

The village's events programme — which runs year-round and encompasses everything from trail races to traditional festivals — depends heavily on volunteers to function. Without local and visitor support, many of the events that give Saas-Fee its character simply wouldn't happen at the scale they do.

Volunteering opportunities vary by season and event:

- **Trail race marshalling** during the summer running season requires marshals at route checkpoints, finish-line support staff, and logistics volunteers. Even a few hours of marshalling gives you a unique perspective on the race and connects you directly with the event community.
- **Christmas market organisation** involves stall setup and breakdown, logistics support, and stewarding during the market days — a warm and sociable way to spend time in the village during the festive season.
- **Cultural festival support** for events including the Alpaufzug, Swiss National Day, and Corpus Christi processions is coordinated through community associations and the village council.

To find out about current volunteering opportunities, contact the **Tourist Office** or the **Gemeinde Saas-Fee** directly. They maintain up-to-date listings of what's needed and can connect you with the relevant organisers.

## Sports Clubs

Saas-Fee has a lively network of sports clubs rooted in the activities that define the village — and several that might surprise you given the resort's scale.

Active clubs cover **skiing**, **football**, **tennis**, and **hiking**, among others. The ski club has historical depth — competitive skiing is woven into the identity of a community where children learn to ski almost before they learn to walk. The hiking club organises regular group outings to peaks and trails across the Saas Valley and beyond.

For seasonal visitors, some clubs welcome temporary members for the duration of their stay — particularly during the winter and summer peak seasons. This is a genuine way to meet locals and integrate into village social life rather than remaining in the visitor bubble. For permanent residents, full membership across the club network is open and actively encouraged.

Enquire locally, check the notice board at the Gemeinde offices, or ask at the Tourist Office for current club contact details.

## Cultural Associations

Walliser cultural life runs deep in Saas-Fee, and the village's cultural associations are the living expression of that tradition.

The **Trachtengruppe** — the traditional dress group — performs at festivals and community events throughout the year, maintaining the tradition of Walliser folk costume that might otherwise fade in a heavily visited international resort. If you have a genuine interest in folk traditions, the group is occasionally open to visitors who want to learn more.

The **local brass band** is a cornerstone of village celebrations — you'll hear it at National Day, at Christmas, at the Alpaufzug, and at community processions throughout the year. Swiss village brass bands have a long and serious tradition, and Saas-Fee's is no exception.

**Choir and music associations** also operate within the village. Visitors with musical backgrounds — particularly those staying for a full season or longer — are sometimes welcomed to join rehearsals. The best approach is simply to make contact: Swiss community associations are generally warm towards those who show genuine interest and respect for local tradition.

## Learning German & Local Integration

Making an effort with the local language is one of the most effective ways to integrate into any community — and Saas-Fee is no exception. German is the language of everyday life in the village, and even a basic command of it opens doors that remain closed to those who rely entirely on English.

- **German language courses** are available in the Saas Valley for those planning longer stays. Courses range from beginners' classes to more advanced conversation groups, and are often available in intensive formats suited to seasonal workers and new residents.
- The **local dialect, Wallisertitsch**, is a genuinely distinct form of Alemannic German that takes considerable time to master — even native German speakers find it challenging. Don't be discouraged if you can't follow local conversations immediately; standard Swiss German (*Hochdeutsch*) is universally understood and genuinely appreciated when used.
- The community responds warmly to those who make an effort to engage with local customs, language, and traditions. A greeting in German, a willingness to join in at community events, and a respectful interest in Walliser culture go a long way.

## Seasonal Worker Community

Saas-Fee has a significant and vibrant seasonal workforce, and that community is very much part of the fabric of village life. Both the winter season (October–April) and the summer season (June–September) bring international workers to the resort, recruited by hotels, the ski area, ski schools, and outdoor guiding companies.

Seasonal workers bring energy, diversity, and skills to the village — and many return year after year, building long-term connections with Saas-Fee that eventually lead to permanent residency. The seasonal worker community has its own social life: regular gatherings, shared staff accommodation, and the particular camaraderie of people living and working together in an extraordinary environment.

If you're coming to Saas-Fee as a seasonal worker, you'll find the community welcoming and well-organised. Most employers provide accommodation or assistance finding it, and the social infrastructure of the worker community means you're unlikely to feel isolated — even if you arrive knowing no one.
