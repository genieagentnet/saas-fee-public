For visitors staying longer than a week, or for new residents settling into Saas-Fee, understanding the local services available is essential. From healthcare and schools to sports facilities and community centres, Saas-Fee punches above its weight for a village of its size. This guide covers the practical infrastructure of village life — the services that matter when you're here not just for a holiday, but as part of the community.

## Healthcare

Medical care in Saas-Fee is well organised for a small alpine village, though for specialist treatment or emergencies requiring hospitalisation, you'll need to travel to the valley.

- **Local Medical Centre (Arztpraxis):** The village has a GP practice offering general consultations, minor treatments, and referrals. For non-emergency medical needs, this is your first port of call. The medical centre handles a wide range of everyday health concerns, including ski and sports injuries.
- **Nearest Hospital:** The nearest full-service hospital is in **Visp**, approximately 45 minutes away by car or bus. Visp's Spital Oberwallis serves the Upper Valais region and has emergency, surgical, and specialist departments.
- **Mountain Rescue:** In the event of a mountain emergency, dial **1414** to reach REGA, the Swiss air rescue service. REGA operates helicopter rescue across Switzerland year-round. Response times in the Saas-Fee area are generally fast, but mountain rescue can be costly — travel insurance covering mountain rescue is essential for all visitors engaging in skiing, hiking, or climbing.
- **Dental Care:** Dentists are based in **Visp** rather than in the village itself. If you anticipate needing dental treatment during an extended stay, it is worth registering with a practice in Visp on arrival.
- **Pharmacy (Apotheke):** A pharmacy in the village centre stocks prescription medications, over-the-counter medicines, and basic medical supplies. The pharmacist can provide advice on minor ailments and direct you appropriately for more serious concerns.
- **European Health Insurance Card (EHIC):** EU and EEA citizens should carry their EHIC, which provides access to medically necessary state-provided healthcare during a stay in Switzerland. Note that Switzerland is not an EU member, but bilateral agreements cover EHIC use for urgent treatment.
- **Travel Insurance:** Private travel insurance is strongly recommended for all visitors. Standard policies should cover medical treatment, mountain rescue, and — for active visitors — sports activities including skiing and hiking at altitude. Check your policy carefully before departure.

## Schools & Childcare

Saas-Fee supports families both resident and visiting with a range of educational and childcare options appropriate to the size and character of the village.

- **Local Primary School:** Resident children attend the village primary school, which follows the Swiss curriculum in German. The school is well integrated into village life, and many community events involve the school in some capacity.
- **Secondary Education:** Secondary school students travel to **Saas-Grund** or **Visp** for their education. Public transport connections make this practical, and many families treat the daily journey as a normal part of upper-valley life.
- **Visiting Children — Ski School Programmes:** For families visiting during the ski season, the Saas-Fee Ski and Snowboard School runs structured programmes for children from age three upwards. These combine instruction with social activities and provide excellent supervised engagement for young skiers while parents ski independently.
- **Summer Camps:** During the summer season, supervised camp programmes for visiting children are available, combining outdoor activities, glacier experiences, and supervised play.
- **Holiday Childcare:** A number of hotels and the ski school offer peak-season childcare for visiting families. Availability varies — enquire directly with your accommodation provider or the ski school when booking.

## Sports & Community Facilities

Saas-Fee's reputation as an outdoor destination extends beyond the mountain. The village maintains a range of sports and community facilities for both residents and longer-stay visitors.

- **Indoor Sports Hall:** The village sports hall hosts community sport, school events, and recreational activities throughout the year.
- **Tennis Courts:** Available during the summer season for casual and club play.
- **Ice Skating Rink:** An outdoor ice rink operates in the village centre through the winter season, offering a fun and accessible activity for all ages.
- **Swimming:** An indoor swimming pool is available, with some hotels offering day visitor access to their pool facilities — enquire with your accommodation provider or the Tourist Office for current access arrangements.
- **Saas-Fee Sportarena:** The village's main community sports and recreation hub, hosting group activities, fitness classes, and recreational sport for residents and guests. It's a good place to connect with the local community outside of the mountain environment.

## Libraries & Cultural

- **Village Library:** Saas-Fee has a community library serving residents. The collection is primarily in German, but international publications are also available.
- **Saaser Museum:** The museum documents the history and culture of the Saas Valley, from traditional farming and crafts to the history of mountaineering and the development of the resort. Regular temporary exhibitions complement the permanent collection. It's an excellent resource for anyone wanting to understand the community they're staying in.
- **Community Events & Cultural Evenings:** The village hosts regular cultural evenings, talks, and community gatherings throughout the year — particularly during the winter and summer peak seasons. Check the Tourist Office board and the village website for current listings.

## Religious Services

The Roman Catholic church in the village centre holds regular services throughout the week and on Sundays. The service schedule is posted on the church notice board and on the village website. The church is open for private reflection outside of service times and is one of the most characterful buildings in the village.

Visitors of other faiths will need to travel to Visp or Brig for non-Catholic services, where a wider range of religious communities is represented.
